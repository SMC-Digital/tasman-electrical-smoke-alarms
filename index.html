What Does A Compliant Smoke Alarm System Require?

Queensland’s requirements come down to the right alarms, in the right places, installed correctly.

Photoelectric Detection

Designed to detect smoke using photoelectric technology.

Interconnected Alarms

When one alarm activates, the alarms throughout the home sound together.

Correct Placement

Alarms may be required in bedrooms, hallways, each storey and appropriate exit paths.

Approved Equipment

Alarms must meet the applicable Australian Standard, AS 3786:2014.

Smoke Alarm Questions, Answered

Clear, plain-English answers to what Brisbane homeowners ask most.

When is the Queensland smoke alarm deadline?
All existing private homes, townhouses, units and manufactured homes in Queensland will need compliant interconnected photoelectric smoke alarms by 1 January 2027. New builds and substantially renovated homes already have to meet the standard. Tasman Electrical can inspect your home and advise what is required.
What type of smoke alarms are required?
Compliant alarms must be photoelectric and comply with Australian Standard AS 3786:2014. They also need to be interconnected with the other alarms in the dwelling.
Do smoke alarms need to be interconnected?
Yes. Alarms must be interconnected so that when one alarm detects smoke, all alarms in the property sound together, giving everyone in the home earlier warning.
Where do smoke alarms need to be installed?
Smoke alarms are generally required on every storey, in each bedroom, and in hallways connecting bedrooms to the rest of the property. Where there is no hallway, an alarm may be required between the bedroom and the remainder of the storey. A storey without bedrooms requires an alarm along the most likely exit path. Exact placement should be confirmed for your property.
Do alarms need to be installed inside every bedroom?
Yes, a compliant system requires a smoke alarm in each bedroom, in addition to hallways and other required locations. A site assessment confirms the exact number and positions for your home.
How do I know how old my smoke alarms are?
Most alarms have a manufacture or expiry date printed on the casing, often near the battery compartment. If you cannot find it or are unsure, Tasman Electrical can check your alarms during an inspection.
Do smoke alarms need to be replaced after 10 years?
Smoke alarms that have expired, stopped working or reached the end of their service life may need replacement. Many alarms have a service life of around 10 years, so older alarms often need replacing.
Can I install smoke alarms myself?
Hardwired smoke alarm electrical work must be completed by an appropriately licensed electrician. Tasman Electrical is a licensed electrical contractor and can install and replace hardwired alarms correctly.
What happens when one interconnected alarm detects smoke?
When one interconnected alarm detects smoke, all of the alarms in the property sound together. This means you are alerted no matter which part of the home the smoke is detected in.
Do I need compliant alarms before selling my property?
Smoke alarm requirements can apply when selling or leasing a property. Tasman Electrical can assess your current alarms and recommend the upgrade required. Property-specific requirements should be confirmed through an inspection.
Can Tasman Electrical inspect my existing smoke alarms?
Yes. Tasman Electrical can inspect your property, identify what is currently installed and recommend the appropriate upgrade or replacement for your home.
How much does smoke alarm installation cost?
The price depends on the property, the number of alarms, the existing wiring, the alarm type and the installation conditions. Request a quote and Tasman Electrical will provide a clear recommendation for your home.
